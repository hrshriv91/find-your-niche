Short, practical guides for the questions that can feel big when you’re holding them on your own.

COMING SOON · EXPLORING⌁

How to explore a career path without committing

A short shadow, conversation, or sample task can answer specific questions, though none guarantees the work will suit you. Pick one low-cost test and note what you learn.

A guide for trying things out
COMING SOON · REASSURANCE☼

What to do when you feel behind

Timelines vary, but prerequisites, fees, and application dates are real constraints. List the dates and credits that apply to you, then ask an adviser which options remain open.

A guide for finding perspective
COMING SOON · STUDYING◌

Switching majors: a survival guide

A change can affect credits, graduation time, financial aid, or visa status. Request a degree audit and aid review first, and ask whether electives or a minor could meet the same need.

A guide for considering a change
COMING SOON · SELF-DISCOVERY✳

How to notice your strengths

Look for evidence in completed work, specific feedback, or tasks you learned quickly. Ask two people for one example, then look for roles that use that skill.

A guide for seeing what’s already there
COMING SOON · INTERESTS✧

When you have too many interests

You do not have to turn every interest into a job; income, training, and daily tasks differ by path. Choose one low-cost project to test this month, and keep another interest outside work if that fits.

A guide for following a thread
COMING SOON · CONVERSATIONS↗

How to talk to someone about their career

One person’s route is an example, not a forecast of yours. Ask about a typical week, entry requirements, trade-offs, and what they would test before investing in a course.

A guide for learning from others
